Important details

  1. Permission vs tax vs fee vs reporting are four different things. Chinese capital controls decide whether you may convert/remit; PRC income tax decides tax on what created the money; bank fees/FX spread decide what the wire costs; US reporting decides what you must file. Confusing them is why the balance looks taxed. T17 T25 T02 T32
  2. Documented current-account income has no annual cap. Art. 59 (T04) allows purchase with ID + source materials incl. tax vouchers. The $50,000 figure is a convenience amount for ID-only processing, not a cap (T06). T04 T06
  3. Bank cost is small; tax/reporting exposure is large. Convert+wire $53.37 on $10k (0.53%), $898.75 on $400k (0.22%) BOC-side. Dividend tax alone is $80,000 (20%) or $40,000 (10% treaty cap) on $400k gross. Willful FBAR max is greater of $165,353 or 50% of balance. T01 T02 T25 T27 T32
  4. US currency gain is the hidden conversion tax. If CNY was acquired at the 2024 IRS average (7.189), converting $400,000 at today's ask recognises ~$27,926 gain (illustrative). Personal gain <=$200 ignored; >$200 capital gain (T31). Actual tax depends on character + marginal rate - blank until advised. T26 T31
  5. Gift/undocumented money has no verified route. Do not invent a purpose, use relatives' quotas, private swaps, crypto, or borrowed cards. Fines reach 100% (Order 532). T04 T17 T21 T23
