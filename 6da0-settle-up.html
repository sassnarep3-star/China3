If tax on the money was never paid: what settling up costs

For a U.S. citizen with RMB in a Chinese bank account. The dashboard says unpaid Chinese tax is "the large number". This page takes that number apart, layer by layer, on both sides, from the laws themselves as read on 11 November 2026.

This is a scale, not your figure. The brief does not say where the money came from or whether tax was withheld, so nothing here is a finding about this account. See F01.

1. The tax is only the first layer

Chinese tax that is paid late grows by 0.05% a day, which is 18.25% of the tax per year. A fine of half the tax to five times the tax can be added. On $100,000 of income taxed at 20% that is $20,000 of tax, $30,950 if paid three years late, and a possible fine of $10,000 to $100,000.

2. Why it was unpaid decides how far back

The tax office's own mistake: three years, no surcharge. Never filed: generally three years, up to five. Evasion: no limit. Not having filed is not evasion; refusing to file after the tax office tells you to is.

C01.52 C03 C01.63 · China rules

3. Only the tax itself comes back

Chinese income tax can be credited against U.S. tax on the same income. The surcharge and the fine cannot. For a cash-method taxpayer the credit belongs to the year you pay, which may not be the year the U.S. taxed the income.

U11 U20 · What comes back

4. In the U.S. the procedure matters more than the tax

If the income and the accounts were reported, there is nothing to settle. If not, and it was not willful, the penalty is nothing for someone living abroad and 5% of the highest balance for someone living in the U.S. Outside those procedures the ceiling is $16,536 for each missed yearly report, or for a willful case the greater of $165,353 or half the balance.
