Executive summary

Important details first, for a U.S. citizen whose RMB balance sits in a Chinese bank account. Every figure links to the official page it was read from.

The four things that actually matter

  1. There is no Chinese "conversion tax". No source read here taxes the act of converting RMB to USD or wiring it out. State Council Regulation Art. 5 says ordinary international payments and transfers are not restricted; the bank asks for proof that Chinese tax on the money was already dealt with.
  2. The bank's own cost is about half a percent or less. At Bank of China's published board and tariff: $53.38 on $10,000 (0.53%) falling to $899.01 on $400,000 (0.22%) because the 0.1% wire fee is capped at RMB 260. Other banks' intermediary and receiving fees are not published and are left blank.
  3. The U.S. side can cost more than the bank. Selling RMB is a currency sale under 26 U.S.C. §988. RMB bought at the 2024 IRS yearly average would show a gain of about 7.0% today — roughly $28,000 on $400,000 — taxed at your ordinary rate unless the personal-transaction rule applies. Reporting penalties are separate: up to $16,536 for an FBAR violation and the willful ceiling is the greater of $165,353 or 50% of the balance.
  4. The route depends on where the money came from. A U.S. passport holder does not get the $50,000-a-year convenience amount on ID alone. Each lawful route is tied to a kind of money: China income, a home sale, an inheritance, pre-emigration property, or dollars already in a foreign-currency account.

Least-tax, least-penalty path (general)

  1. Classify the money. Where did each part of the balance come from, and was Chinese tax paid on it? (flag U01)
  2. Fix U.S. reporting first. FBAR if foreign accounts exceeded $10,000 at any time in the year; Form 8938 above its thresholds; report any currency gain. Missed reports carry independent penalties.
  3. Use the cheapest lawful route that fits. Dollars already in a Chinese foreign-currency account: wire out (L03, $61.33 flat above the cap). Documented China income: buy USD with source documents and tax evidence, one wire (L01, $53.38 on $10,000, $899.01 on $400,000).
  4. Avoid prohibited routes. Splitting relatives' convenience amounts, private swaps/money changers, false documents, cryptocurrency, and carrying more than $10,000 undeclared are each punishable — fines up to 30% of the amount, 30–100% in serious cases, and possible criminal liability.
