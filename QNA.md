## Section 1 — About the money (decides which route applies)

### Q1. Where did the money in the Chinese bank account come from?

**What we know:** Not stated in the brief. The source of funds selects the lawful route: documented China income, home-sale proceeds, inheritance, emigrant property, RMB you converted from your own foreign currency within 24 months, foreign currency already in the account, or a gift/no documents (no verified route).  
**Official source:** SAFE Tianjin, "2026年外汇管理互联网信息——个人外汇（08）" — https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2026/0713/3088.html  
**What we do not know:** The source of this specific balance.  
**How to find it:** Gather bank statements and the documents behind each large deposit (salary slips, tax returns, sale contract, inheritance papers, original exchange memo). Ask the bank in writing which route applies to your account and account category (境内个人 vs 境外个人).

### Q2. What exactly are the "considerable taxes" the brief refers to?

**What we know:** The brief names no tax, rate, amount, or assessing office. This research found no tax on the act of converting CNY to USD or on wiring the money out. China's Foreign Exchange Regulation says current-account international payments and transfers are not restricted (State Council Order No. 532, Art. 5). The likely candidates are: (a) Chinese income tax on whatever *created* the money (3%–45% wages; 20% dividends/rent/property gains; bank-deposit interest exempt since 2008), and (b) U.S. information-reporting penalties if filings were missed. Neither is confirmed for this case.  
**Official sources:** SAFE FX Regulation, Art. 5 — https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2008/0806/5321.html ; China Individual Income Tax Law — https://www.chinatax.gov.cn/n810219/n810744/n3752930/n3752974/c3970366/content.html ; Savings interest exemption — https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c102416/c5203286/content.html  
**What we do not know:** Which tax (if any) was being quoted, at what rate, and by which office.  
**How to find it:** Request the bank's or tax office's written statement naming the tax, rate, and amount. Until then, treat "considerable taxes" as unverified (flag U02).

### Q3. Is the money yours to move, or is it subject to a Chinese tax that must be paid first?

**What we know:** If Chinese income tax was never paid on the money, it is owed whether or not the money moves — conversion does not create or erase it. Wages 3%–45%; dividends, rent, property gains 20%. Savings-deposit interest is exempt.  
**Official source:** China IIT Law — https://www.chinatax.gov.cn/n810219/n810744/n3752930/n3752974/c3970366/content.html  
**What we do not know:** Whether tax on the underlying income was already paid, and what category the money falls in.  
**How to find it:** Your Chinese tax returns and withholding records; a tax certificate (完税凭证) from the tax office, which several remittance routes require anyway.

## Section 2 — China-side rules and limits

### Q4. Which "individual FX category" does your bank assign you: 境内个人 or 境外个人?

**What we know:** The two governing instruments define it differently. The State Council FX Regulation (Art. 52) counts a foreigner who has lived in China continuously for 1 year as 境内个人; the PBOC Individual FX Measures classify by the ID document used at account opening, with the bank setting the category. A U.S. passport holder does **not** get the USD 50,000-a-year convenience amount on ID alone — only holders of the Chinese Foreign Permanent Resident ID do.  
**Official sources:** SAFE FX Regulation Art. 52 — https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2008/0806/5321.html ; PBOC Individual FX Measures — http://m.safe.gov.cn/safe/2006/1225/5319.html ; SAFE Ningxia Q&A No. 8 — https://www.safe.gov.cn/ningxia/2022/0929/1997.html ; SAFE Tianjin Q&A No. 9 — https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2024/0621/2529.html  
**What we do not know:** Which category your bank applied to your account.  
**How to find it:** Ask the bank in writing for your account's 境内个人/境外个人 classification. This is the single open question (I03 / U10) that decides whether the USD 50,000 figure binds you.

### Q5. Is there a Chinese tax on converting your own CNY principal into USD?

**What we know:** No official source read here imposes a tax on conversion itself or on the wire. What the bank requires is proof that Chinese tax on the underlying income was handled.  
**Official source:** SAFE FX Regulation, Art. 5 — https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2008/0806/5321.html ; SAFE Tianjin personal FX Q&A — https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2026/0713/3088.html  
**What we do not know:** Nothing additional; this is confirmed by the sources above.  
**How to find it:** Not needed — confirmed. The cost of conversion is the bank's spread and fees, not a tax.

### Q6. Can a relative's USD 50,000 annual quota be used to move your money?

**What we know:** No. SAFE expressly prohibits splitting the quota across multiple people or relatives (quota splitting / 分拆结售汇). Using another person's quota is unlawful, with fines up to 30% of the amount and 30%–100% in serious cases, plus criminal liability possible.  
**Official source:** SAFE FX Regulation, Arts. 39–40 — https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2008/0806/5321.html  
**What we do not know:** Nothing additional.  
**How to find it:** Not needed — prohibited; do not use.

### Q7. Can crypto (USDT, etc.) be used to convert CNY to USD?

**What we know:** No. PBOC and eight other agencies declared virtual-currency business illegal financial activity (2021), still illegal under the 2026 notice 银发〔2026〕42号 (which repealed 银发〔2021〕237号). No lawful CNY → USDT → USD route exists.  
**Official source:** PBOC notice — https://www.pbc.gov.cn/tiaofasi/144941/3581332/2026020619591971323/index.html  
**What we do not know:** Nothing additional.  
**How to find it:** Not needed — prohibited; do not use.

### Q9. Is there a cheaper lawful route than one documented-income wire?

**What we know:** If part of the balance is already USD in a foreign-currency account, wire that out first — no spread to pay ($61.33 wire/cable only, any amount). If you converted RMB from your own foreign currency within the last 24 months and kept the exchange slip, change it back (same bank cost as one wire, no quota problem). Otherwise, the cheapest lawful sequence is: classify the money → fix U.S. reporting first → then use the documented-income route (no annual quota for 境外个人 with evidence review) or the emigrant-property-transfer route (no amount ceiling, but staged over two years and needs a cancelled hukou).  
**Official sources:** SAFE Tianjin personal FX Q&A (re-conversion with exchange slip) — https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2026/0917/3134.html ; PBOC Order [2004] No. 16 (emigrant property transfer) — http://m.safe.gov.cn/safe/2004/1116/5461.html  
**What we do not know:** Whether your situation qualifies for L02/L03 (depends on documents you hold).  
**How to find it:** Your original exchange memo (for L02) or foreign-currency account statements (for L03); bank confirmation of eligibility.

## Section 4 — U.S. tax and reporting

### Q10. Is converting CNY to USD a taxable event in the U.S.?

**What we know:** Selling RMB for dollars is a currency transaction under IRC §988. If the CNY appreciated since you acquired it, a U.S. §988 currency gain arises (roughly 6–7% of USD received if CNY was bought at the 2024 IRS yearly average). A personal-transaction exclusion (§988(e)) may apply to gains of $200 or less, but whether a savings balance qualifies is a legal judgment. The conversion itself is not Chinese-taxed income.  
**Official sources:** 26 U.S.C. §988 — https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title26/html/USCODE-2023-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapN-partIII-subpartJ-sec988.htm ; IRS yearly average rates — https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/yearly-average-currency-exchange-rates  
**What we do not know:** The dates/rates at which you acquired the CNY, and whether the personal-transaction exclusion applies to your balance.  
**How to find it:** A U.S. tax professional, with your CNY acquisition dates and rates.

### Q11. What U.S. reporting forms apply to the Chinese account?

**What we know:** FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) if the aggregate value of foreign accounts exceeded $10,000 at any time in the calendar year; Form 8938 if thresholds are met (higher, residency-dependent); Form 3520 for certain foreign gifts. Each missed form carries its own penalty.  
**Official sources:** FBAR/8938 comparison — https://www.irs.gov/businesses/comparison-of-form-8938-and-fbar-requirements ; 31 CFR 1010.821 (FBAR penalties) — https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-H/section-1010.821 ; Form 3520 / foreign gifts — https://www.irs.gov/businesses/gifts-from-foreign-person  
**What we do not know:** Whether you have met these obligations in prior years, and the account's maximum value.  
**How to find it:** Your account statements and prior-year U.S. tax returns; a U.S. tax adviser for any delinquent-filing cure (streamlined procedures, etc.).

### Q12. What is the largest U.S. penalty exposure?

**What we know:** A willful FBAR violation penalty is capped at the greater of $165,353 (2025 amount, inflation-adjusted) or 50% of the account balance — an order of magnitude larger than any FX spread.  
**Official source:** 31 CFR 1010.821, Table 1 (31 U.S.C. 5321(a)(5)(C)(i)(I)) — https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-H/section-1010.821  
**What we do not know:** Your account balance and whether any violation was willful.  
**How to find it:** Account records; a U.S. tax adviser.

### Q14. What are the prohibited routes and what do they cost if caught?

**What we know:** Relatives' quotas, underground banks, crypto conversion, manufactured trade documents, and "gift" routing to evade FX rules are unlawful. Penalties: up to 30% of the amount, 30%–100% in serious cases, plus criminal liability; funds may be ordered returned.  
**Official source:** SAFE FX Regulation, Arts. 39, 40, 45 — https://www.safe.gov.cn/safe/2008/0806/5321.html ; PBOC virtual-currency notice — https://www.pbc.gov.cn/tiaofasi/144941/3581332/2026020619591971323/index.html  
**What we do not know:** Nothing additional.  
**How to find it:** Not needed — do not use.
