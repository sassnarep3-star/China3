There is no Chinese tax on converting your own CNY into USD.
The bill is a bank spread plus a capped wire fee — and, if you cross the line, a reporting penalty.

Every conversion route ends in the same two China-side charges: the bank sells you USD at 6.6851 rather than the mid of its own board, 6.6711, and it charges 1 per mille of the CNY amount (min CNY 50, max CNY 260) plus a CNY 150 cable fee. On today's board that is $53.38 on USD 10,000 (0.5338%) and $899.01 on USD 400,000 (0.2248%) — if one transfer is allowed.

  1. Best route for a normal individual: one documented bank wire. Convert and remit in a single instruction with ID plus source-of-funds or purpose documents. $899.01 all-in on USD 400,000 on today's board. V01 V02 V04
  2. If the bank insists on the USD 50,000 convenience amount, the same USD 400,000 takes 8 transfers and $1,328.33 in fees (0.3321%), because you pay the cable and wire fee each time. V04
  3. If you emigrated and the balance is pre-emigration property, use the emigrant property transfer. SAFE approves the amount; the bank then charges exactly the same spread and wire fee. V07
  4. The expensive number is not the exchange rate. A willful FBAR violation carries a maximum of $165,353.00 per violation in the current table, or 50% of the account balance where that is greater; a serious PRC evasion case is fined 30%–100% of the amount. V13 V12 V05

Key finding: items 1–3 are the entire published cost of a lawful conversion, and they fall as the amount rises because the wire fee is capped. Items 5–9 are where the “considerable tax” in the question really comes from — and none of them is an exchange tax.

The one U.S. tax that does attach to converting CNY

Selling your own currency is a § 988 transaction V16. For an individual's personal transaction, exchange-rate gain is not recognised unless it exceeds $200.00 V15. Above that, the gain is ordinary income, taxed at your own marginal rate — the 24% and 37% columns are arithmetic, not a prediction.

If the CNY was acquired at a higher CNY-per-USD rate than today's 6.6851 — the normal case for savings built up over years — the conversion produces a dollar gain. If it was acquired recently at a similar rate, the gain usually falls under the USD 200 personal-transaction line and nothing is recognised. The PRC side has no equivalent charge on principal: the IIT statute taxes income, at 20% for the categories that apply here V10, and requires a tax clearance only when a person cancels their PRC household registration on emigrating V11.
