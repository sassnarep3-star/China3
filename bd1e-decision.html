Before you decide

A U.S. citizen has RMB in a Chinese bank account and has been told that turning it into dollars means "a considerable amount of taxes". The repository already holds a long-form Q&A register (QNA.md). This page is its site-ready counterpart: the questions that must be answered before any route can be chosen, ordered by what they decide, each with a status and the route it unlocks — plus three open items that were closed today against the official text and the cost ladder recomputed at today's board. Every figure and every statement here carries a link to the official page it was read from on 11 November 2026.

What the sources actually say. No official page read for this project puts a tax on the conversion or on the wire. The Chinese tax that matters is tax on the income behind the money, owed whether or not it moves (BD-S12 IIT Law). The bank is required to see the tax evidence before it sells the dollars (BD-S08 SAFE Tianjin). On the U.S. side the risks are the currency gain on the RMB (BD-S16 IRS rates) and the reporting forms, whose penalties are larger than the bank's fee (BD-S05 31 USC 5321 BD-S06 31 CFR 1010.821).

5. The dividend route and the treaty cap

This is the only place where the treaty changes a number on this site. Domestic law taxes dividends at a flat 20% (BD-S12 IIT Law); the treaty caps Chinese tax at 10% of the gross amount for a beneficial owner who is a U.S. resident (BD-S03 IRS treaty). The relief is not automatic: it is self-assessed under SAT Announcement 2019 No. 35 — the information report form goes to the withholding agent, with a U.S. residency certificate and beneficial-owner evidence kept on file. Without the form, the payer withholds under domestic law (BD-S04 STA 2019/35).

Chinese tax only. The U.S. tax on the same dividend, and the foreign tax credit that may offset it, are not modelled here; this table is not a statement about your combined burden (BD-F01). The 10% cap is also not available to a person who is a Chinese resident under the treaty tie-breaker (Q05).

How the relief is actually claimed

  1. Obtain a certificate of U.S. residency for the year from the IRS — the document the announcement requires (BD-S04 STA 2019/35, Art. 7(1)).
  2. Complete the 《非居民纳税人享受协定待遇信息报告表》 and give it to the company or bank that pays the dividend, before it withholds. If you do not, it withholds 20%.
  3. Keep the ownership documents and the beneficial-owner evidence on file for the statutory retention period; the tax office can demand them later.
  4. If relief is later found not to have been due, declare and pay the shortfall yourself. Improper use creates a compliance record and late-payment liability.
