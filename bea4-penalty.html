1. What is not a penalty

  • Currency conversion. No source read imposes a tax on exchanging RMB for USD. China's Order 532 says the state does not restrict current-account international payments and transfers (S17 Art. 5).
  • The exchange-rate move. For an individual's personal transaction, §988 gain is not recognised unless it exceeds USD 200 — and on these balances it usually does (S27(e)). Where it does exceed USD 200, the ordinary-income treatment applies to the gain; that is income tax on a currency gain, not a toll on transferring.
  • Bank charges. These are a published percentage, not a penalty: 0.1% wire (min CNY 50, max CNY 260) plus CNY 150 cable, and a 0.4186% buy/sell spread on today's board (S02, S01).

4. Three worked cases at USD 100,000

Done properly, documented income

Cost 270.75 — 0.27% of the amount — made up of the bank spread and the wire tariff. No Chinese tax on the conversion; no U.S. tax on the conversion. Reporting duties still apply: an FBAR once the account exceeds USD 10,000 at any time, and Form 8938 once the thresholds are met.

S30 FBAR, S29 8938

Done through someone else's USD 50,000 amount

Splitting is expressly prohibited, and the people involved go on a watch list for the year and the next two. If it is treated as evading FX controls, the fine ceiling is 30% of the amount — USD 30,000 here — rising to 30–100% in serious cases.

S21, S10 watch list, S17

Transferred lawfully, account never reported

The transfer can be perfectly lawful and the U.S. exposure still dwarfs it: non-willful FBAR up to USD 16,536 per violation; willful up to USD 165,353 (greater of USD 165,353 or 50% of the balance); Form 8938 up to USD 60,000.

S31, S29

5. Limits of this page

  • Every percentage is quoted from the official page linked in the row. The multiplication is this site's own arithmetic and is reproducible from data/bea4_penalty_exposure.csv.
  • Penalties are discretionary and fact-specific. Whether conduct is 逃汇, 套汇 or neither is decided by the FX bureau on the documents, not by this table. Nothing here is a prediction.
  • The columns are alternatives, not cumulative: a single transfer cannot attract both a 30% fine and a 100% fine, and Chinese and U.S. exposures arise from different conduct.
  • Chinese tax on the underlying income is a separate question and is blank wherever the facts (original cost, gain) are not in the repository. See Tax and Flagged.
  • General research, not tax or legal advice.
