Reconciliation and repository audit

This repository contains fifteen parallel editions. They publish different headline figures for the same transfer. This page sets every one of them side by side, states the exact basis of each, and says which are current, which are superseded by a newer board, and which are genuinely different because they price a different scenario.

The short version

Most of the apparent disagreement is not disagreement. Of the eighteen figures compared, the great majority reproduce exactly once you know two things: which board snapshot was frozen, and whether one wire or eight was being priced. The headline figures move by about two dollars on USD 10,000 between board snapshots taken hours apart (F10), and the eight-wire scenario legitimately costs about USD 429 more than one wire at USD 400,000 because the cable fee is paid eight times.

Two things genuinely were wrong, and both are recorded rather than quietly fixed:

  1. methods.html states the USD 50,000 convenience amount as a legal cap and a purchase quota available to an individual. SAFE states there is no amount limit for genuine current-account business and that the annual amount belongs to domestic individuals and Foreign Permanent Resident ID holders C04 C05. See F08.
  2. Several editions publish a "full spread" column that charges both halves of the bid-ask spread on a one-sided conversion. Where it is clearly labelled as a sensitivity it is honest; a reader who takes it as the answer overstates the cost by about USD 20.94 per USD 10,000 and USD 837.43 per USD 400,000. See F01.

How these verdicts were reached

Each page's own stated formula was read from the page, not assumed. Two hypotheses were tested and rejected during this work, and are recorded because a rejected hypothesis is part of an honest audit:

  • "53.47 and 53.42 must double-count the spread." Rejected: 53.38 and 53.42 reproduce exactly as spread + wire under the same one-sided convention, on two different board snapshots. The difference is the board, not the method.
  • "76e's 53.42 and 901.20 use a full-spread convention." Rejected: 76e-verification.html states its convention in words — spread = amount × (ask − (bid + ask) / 2) / ask — which is the same one-sided half-spread used everywhere else. The figures differ from today's only by the board.

What survives is the smaller, checkable set of findings above, each with the page, the basis and a source link.

What this session verified, and how

Twenty-two official pages were fetched and read on 2026-11-11, each recorded with its URL, the UTC timestamp of the read, and a verbatim quotation in Sources. Three further rows are marked not read this session and are used only with that flag.

The arithmetic is recomputed twice: once by scripts/c348_engine.py and once, independently and with exact fractions, by scripts/verify_c348.py, which does not import the builder. The verifier also checks that every row carries an official link, that every blank names a flag, that every internal link and anchor resolves, and that the figures printed on these pages equal the figures in the CSV. It runs in CI on every push.

What this edition is. An additive, independently verified edition built in its own file namespace so it collides with no other session's work. It does not edit the canonical dashboard. Figures are recomputed by scripts/c348_engine.py and re-derived from scratch by scripts/verify_c348.py (which shares no code with the builder).

What it is not. Not tax, legal or investment advice. Every blank cell is a cell no official page read in this pass fills; blanks are named in the tables and listed on Flagged for review. Rates move: the board timestamp is printed beside each figure.
