The shape of the curve. The wire fee is 1 per mille of the amount with a CNY 260 ceiling, so it stops growing at about USD 37,300 and becomes a flat CNY 410 (CNY 260 + CNY 150 cable). Above that, only the spread grows, and it grows linearly. That is why the cost percentage falls from 0.53% to 0.22% as the amount rises. It is arithmetic on the published tariff, not advice.

3. How the figures are computed, so you can check them

Spread. amount × (selling rate − midpoint) ÷ selling rate, where midpoint is the arithmetic mean of the board's own buying and selling rates. On today's board: (6.6871 − 6.6731) ÷ 6.6871 = 0.2093%.

Wire tariff. min(CNY 260, max(CNY 50, CNY amount × 0.001)) + CNY 150, converted to USD at the selling rate. One wire is one tariff; eight wires are eight tariffs.

Total. The components are rounded to cents before they are added, so that the columns on this page add up to the total shown. The difference from adding first and rounding later is at most one cent.

Blank means blank. The U.S. receiving and intermediary fees are not published for a personal wire, so they are never counted as zero F03. That makes every total here a known cost and a lower bound, not an all-in quote.

5. What changes the answer

  • One wire versus tranches. Pricing USD 400,000 as eight USD 50,000 wires ($1,327.93) instead of one costs $1,327.93 − $898.74 more, and takes eight calendar years.
  • Whether the balance is already USD — the spread vanishes: $61.31 against $898.74.
  • The rate. The board moved intraday and every prior edition froze a different snapshot; the spread between those snapshots is about USD 2 per USD 10,000 F10.
