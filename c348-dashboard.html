What it actually costs to get money out of China

A U.S. citizen's balance in a Chinese bank, priced from the official Bank of China board and tariff, the SAFE rules and the U.S. tax code — every cell recomputed, every gap named. Read the five findings, then the table, then the questions.

The answer in one paragraph

The transfer itself is not heavily taxed. No official source read in this pass imposes a tax or a penalty on converting RMB and wiring it out with documents. What you pay is the bank's spread and wire fee — about $53.37 on USD 10,000 (0.53%) and $898.74 on USD 400,000 (0.22%) at Bank of China. The money at stake is elsewhere: (1) whether the money can be documented at all, which decides whether any lawful route exists; (2) the U.S. currency gain, $27,926.00 on USD 400,000 if the RMB was acquired in 2024, taxed at 15% or more; and (3) the penalties for getting the reporting wrong, which are $120,000.00 under PRC Art. 39 for a "move it however you can" approach and up to $200,000.00 for a willful FBAR failure — roughly 222 times the bank cost.

Five findings that decide everything

  1. The USD 50,000 figure is not a cap. SAFE states there is no amount limit for genuine current-account business; above USD 50,000 the transaction simply needs documents instead of an ID alone C05. One page in this repository states this backwards (F08).
  2. A U.S. passport holder does not get the USD 50,000 convenience amount. It belongs to domestic individuals and to holders of the PRC Foreign Permanent Resident ID C04 C05. For a foreign individual the route is Article 59: ID plus source-of-funds documents including tax evidence C03.
  3. If the balance is already USD, the spread disappears. Wiring existing foreign currency costs only the wire tariff: $61.31 on USD 400,000 C22 C02. Whether the balance is RMB or USD is question Q01 and it is not recorded anywhere.
  4. The U.S. tax is on the currency gain, not on the money. Gain = dollars received minus the dollar basis; the 2021-to-2024 swing on USD 400,000 is a USD 14,575 loss against a USD 27,926 gain C11 C15. Whether that gain is capital or ordinary is genuinely unresolved (F07) and worth about USD 6,144 at USD 400,000.
  5. Doing it unlawfully costs 100 times more than doing it lawfully. $120,000.00 against $898.74 C06. The cheap routes that "avoid tax" are the offences the rules exist to stop.
