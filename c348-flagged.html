F01 — Several editions charge the full bid-ask spread on a one-sided conversion high open

What was foundMultiple pages derive the FX cost as amount x (ask - bid) / ask - both halves of the spread - when a customer buying USD pays only the selling side. On today's board that is 0.4186% instead of 0.2093%, i.e. about USD 20.94 extra per USD 10,000 and USD 837.43 extra per USD 400,000.
WhereThe full-spread convention appears on c5bf-rates.html (labelled sensitivity and delta columns), m5f19-costs.html (sensitivity column) and the 76e-verification table. The one-sided convention is used on index.html, d646, a513, b9b3, n676 and this edition.
Official source bearing on itC01 (the board prints Buying 665.91 and Selling 668.71; the customer buys at the Selling rate only); C02 (the tariff is on top of the rate) C01 C02
What it changesUp to USD 20.94 per USD 10,000 and USD 837.43 per USD 400,000 of the published headline
How to close itDecide one convention repo-wide and re-label every sensitivity column as "not the answer". This edition prices the one-sided cost and shows the full-spread column side by side in the reconciliation table so the gap is never invisible.

F07 — What a personal currency gain is taxed as is the single most consequential open question high open

What was found26 U.S.C. 988(a)(1)(A) makes foreign currency gain ordinary. 988(e)(1) removes a personal transaction from the ordinary rule, and IRS Publication 525 (C12) says a personal gain above USD 200 is reported as a capital gain. The statutes and the IRS publication therefore point to different characters, and the difference on USD 400,000 is real: at a 37% ordinary rate the tax on USD 27,926 of gain is USD 10,333; at 15% it is USD 4,189.
WhereThis edition prices the capital-gain reading and states the ordinary alternative in the tax page and in every tax row
Official source bearing on itC11 (988(a)(1)(A), 988(e)(1)-(3)); C12 (Publication 525); C13 (rates) C11 C12 C13
What it changesUp to about USD 6,144 on a USD 400,000 balance acquired in 2024
How to close itA written opinion from a U.S. tax adviser on the specific facts, or an IRS private letter ruling. Until then, plan for the capital treatment and reserve the difference.

F08 — One page in the repository states the USD 50,000 convenience amount backwards high open

What was foundmethods.html says "Legal $50,000 USD-equivalent per person per year (individual purchase quota)" and lists "$50,000 cap (non-salary funds)" as a drawback. SAFE states the opposite: the amount is a convenience threshold, and there is no amount limit for genuine current-account business; above the threshold the transaction simply needs documents (C05). The page also presents the quota as available to a generic individual, whereas the annual convenience amount is for domestic individuals and for holders of the PRC Foreign Permanent Resident ID (C04, C05).
Wheremethods.html, route table, row "1 Bank FX purchase (CNY→USD) + international wire"
Official source bearing on itC04; C05; C03 (Art. 59 is the route for a foreign individual) C04 C05 C03
What it changesA reader who believes it cannot plan at all - it would wrongly cap the move at USD 50,000 a year
How to close itThe owner of methods.html should replace the quote with the C05 wording. This session does not edit another edition's page; the correction is requested in COORDINATION.md.
