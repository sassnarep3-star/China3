What the rules cost when they are broken

This is the table that answers the real question. The lawful route on USD 400,000 costs $898.74. The unlawful ones cost up to $120,000.00 on the Chinese side and up to $200,000.00 on the U.S. side — 223 times the bank cost.

The comparison that matters. A lawful, documented conversion of USD 400,000 costs about $898.74 in bank spread and wire fee C01 C02. Evading China's FX rules carries a fine of up to $120,000.00 on the same amount — up to 30% of the sum involved C06. A willful FBAR failure on a USD 400,000 balance carries up to $200,000.00 — 50% of the balance C17. Every "cheap" route that avoids tax is priced against these numbers, not against the $898.74.

What is not a penalty

The exchange spread, the wire fee and the cable fee are prices, not penalties, and they are not taxes. Nothing in the official texts read for this edition makes the conversion of lawful, documented money a taxable event on the Chinese side. The tax that does exist is on the income behind the money, and on the currency gain on the U.S. side. If you have been told the transfer itself carries "a considerable amount of tax", that statement is not supported by any source read here — it may be about unpaid PRC income tax, which is a different thing (P06, and question Q03).
