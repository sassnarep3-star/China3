Executive summary

What is known, what it costs, what remains unknown, and what to do next — most important first.

1. The direct answer to the question asked

Least-tax lawful way, on the evidence available: convert through a bank against documented source-of-funds and tax evidence (method M01) and wire once. It is the only route that the official texts support for a foreign individual's balance without an eligibility condition C03 C06. Its known cost is $53.37 on USD 10,000 and $898.74 on USD 400,000.

If the balance is already in USD, wire it without converting (M02): $61.31 on USD 400,000, and no currency gain arises on the wire itself.

If the money cannot be documented, this edition finds no verified lawful route and says so rather than inventing one. That is question Q02, and it is the first thing to establish.

5. What to do, in order

  1. Establish the currency of the balance (Q01) and the documents behind it (Q02). Everything else depends on these two.
  2. Confirm whether any FBAR was due and filed for past years (Q07). Fixing reporting before moving money is cheaper than explaining it afterwards.
  3. Collect the acquisition dates and rates for the balance (Q05), because they set the U.S. tax.
  4. Choose between M01 (documented conversion) and M02 (wire of existing USD) — nothing else is priced as a complete route for a foreign individual.
  5. Get the receiving bank's incoming-wire fee in writing (Q09), then send one small test wire.
  6. Get a written opinion on the character of the gain (Q06, F07) before filing.

6. Honest limits of this edition

  • The U.S. receiving-bank and intermediary fees are not verified (F03).
  • The cross-border RMB route is not priced at all (F02).
  • The ATM tariff and the banknote-carrying threshold are inherited, not re-read (F04, F05).
  • The PRC property-tax and individual-income-tax rates were not re-read in this pass; no figure from them is used.
  • The capital-versus-ordinary character of a personal gain is unresolved on the official texts (F07).
