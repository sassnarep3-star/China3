$10,000 to $400,000, in $10,000 steps

The only China-side prices an official document publishes are the FX spread and the wire tariff. Both are used here exactly as published: 0.2094% half-spread on the ask (S01) and 1‰ of the CNY amount, minimum ¥50, maximum ¥260, plus ¥150 cable (S02). Anything the sources do not publish is blank and flagged — never zero.

Two ways to count the fee, both shown. “One transfer” applies the tariff once. “Split at $50,000” applies it to each whole $50,000 transfer. The split costs more at every amount above $50,000 and is a fee assumption, not a legal requirement: SAFE's rule is that above $50,000 a day you produce documents, not that you must break the payment up (S03).

Why the percentage falls as the amount rises

The ¥260 cap is the reason. At $10,000 the fee is driven by the 1‰ rate (¥66.85, so ¥216.85 with cable); by $38,862 the cap binds and every additional dollar costs only the spread. The headline rate therefore falls from 0.5338% to 0.2248% — which is exactly why the cheapest large transfer is one transfer, and why splitting into eight adds 429.32 dollars at $400,000 for no legal benefit.
