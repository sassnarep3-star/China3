Method catalogue · 14 routes

Every route, and what is actually wrong with it

Four of these are lawful, documented bank routes that differ mainly in which documents you can produce. One is an offshore-account route that does not solve the outbound leg. Two are investment or trade channels that are not conversions of an existing balance. Three are prohibited, and they are here only so they can be recognised.

Reading the prohibited entries. Nothing in this repository is a how-to for M12–M14. The cost of those routes is not a fee but a fine of up to 30% of the amount, or 30%–100% where serious (S05 arts. 39, 40, 45), so they carry no price and no steps.

M01 · Documented current-account bank remittance (one transfer)

Lawful Bank
Who can use it
Any holder of a mainland CNY account whose money is lawful current-account income, with ID and source documents. A foreign passport holder needs source-of-funds materials including tax documents (S04).
Pros
Cheapest published price found: bank spread plus the 1‰ tariff. Bank files the FX and balance-of-payments return; the paper trail is what keeps you clear of art. 39 (S05). Above USD 50,000/day you add documents, not permission (S03).
Cons
Requires documents you may not have. Only the China-side cost is published — the U.S. receiving and any correspondent deduction are blank (F06).
PRC tax
No PRC tax on the conversion itself was identified; PRC tax attaches to the underlying income, if any (S08).
U.S. tax
§988 ordinary gain on disposing of the CNY deposit, with the USD 200 personal-transaction ceiling (S09).
Priced?
Yes — published tariff

M02 · Same remittance, split into whole USD 50,000 transfers

Lawful Bank
Who can use it
Same as M01.
Pros
Stays inside the amount that needs no transaction documents (S03), which some branches find easier to process.
Cons
Pays the CNY 260 cap plus CNY 150 cable on every transfer, so the fee scales with the number of wires. On today's tariff that is up to 8 × CNY 410 at USD 400,000. It is a fee-maximising assumption, not a legal requirement: S03 says above USD 50,000 you produce documents.
PRC tax
As M01.
U.S. tax
As M01, but each transfer is its own §988 transaction — the USD 200 exclusion is tested per transaction (S09).
Priced?
Yes — published tariff

M03 · Emigrant property transfer (移民财产转移)

Lawful with SAFE approval Capital account
Who can use it
A natural person who has moved from the mainland to a foreign country and who converts property they owned BEFORE acquiring emigrant status. Needs cancelled household registration, proof of settlement abroad, property-rights documents and a PRC tax certificate (S06 art. 8).
Pros
Built for exactly this problem: the whole pre-emigration estate, converted and remitted once. The staging requirement and the over-RMB-500,000 head-office filing were both cancelled in 2014, so the bank may remit the approved amount in one lump (S07).
Cons
Approval is a SAFE decision, not an entitlement, and the tax certificate must come first. A U.S. citizen who never held mainland household registration does not obviously meet art. 2's definition of an emigrant, so this route may be closed to them (flag F08). Art. 11 forbids paying it out as domestic FX cash.
PRC tax
20% on property-transfer income, base = proceeds less original value and reasonable expenses (S08 art. 6(5)); settled before household registration is cancelled (S08 art. 13).
U.S. tax
§988 on the conversion; the property gain itself is separately U.S. taxable and may carry a foreign tax credit (S08 art. 7 is the PRC side of that).
Priced?
Yes — published tariff

M04 · Inheritance transfer (继承财产转移)

Lawful with SAFE approval Capital account
Who can use it
A foreign citizen or HK/Macau resident who inherited mainland property and has realised it. Needs proof of inheritance, the deceased's property documents and a tax certificate (S06 art. 9).
Pros
The route designed for a foreign heir; there is no household-registration requirement. Since 2014, applications relating to different deceased persons may be combined (S07 §6(3)).
Cons
SAFE approval at the deceased's last registered locality; notarisation of the inheritance and property documents is still required (S06 art. 9). Price at the bank is the same tariff, but nothing about this route is fast.
PRC tax
PRC tax on the deceased's or the heir's gain depends on facts not in evidence here (F04).
U.S. tax
§988 on conversion; a U.S. inheritance is generally not income, but the FX gain is (S09).
Priced?
Yes — published tariff

Lawful Bank

M06 · Foreign passport holder without a PR ID — documented income purchase

Lawful, document-driven Bank
Who can use it
Lawful current-account RMB income earned in China, with ID and source-of-funds materials including tax documents (S04).
Pros
Open to a U.S. citizen who worked in China and can show the income and the tax paid. No approval file, no cap in the article quoted.
Cons
The bank decides whether your documents establish a current-account income; the same page that grants PR-ID holders the convenience amount withholds it from everyone else. If the balance is savings or capital rather than documented income, this article does not authorise it.
PRC tax
PRC tax was already withheld or settled on the income; the conversion adds none (S08).
U.S. tax
As M01.

M07 · Overseas ATM / card cash withdrawal

Lawful up to the cap Card
Who can use it
Holder of a mainland-issued card.
Pros
No SAFE application; the money arrives as cash. Hard cap published: CNY 100,000 equivalent per person per calendar year, CNY 10,000 per card per day (S15).
Cons
The cap is about USD 14,960 a year at today's ask — nowhere near USD 400,000. The issuer's FX conversion rate and any ATM operator fee are not published in the sources reviewed, so the price column is blank, not zero (F05). Exceeding the cap suspends the facility for the current and the following year (S15).
PRC tax
None identified on the withdrawal itself.
U.S. tax
Cash in hand is still a §988 disposition when the currency leaves your account (S09).
Priced?
No — blank and flagged (F05)

M08 · Carry cash across the border

Lawful only within the undeclared limit and with U.S. reporting Cash
Who can use it
Anyone, within the PRC export limit and with a FinCEN Form 105 on the U.S. side above USD 10,000.
Pros
No wire fee. The U.S. side is a report, not a tax (S14).
Cons
Two legal systems at once. The PRC limit is set by SAFE under art. 15 of S05 but the figure was not re-verified in this pass, so it is deliberately blank (F05) rather than restated from memory. Failure to file the U.S. report risks seizure and forfeiture (S14). Art. 42 of S05 fines illegal carriage up to 20% of the amount.
PRC tax
None identified on the carriage itself.
U.S. tax
§988 gain is realised when the currency is disposed of, not when it is carried (S09).
Priced?
No — blank and flagged (F05)

M09 · Offshore CNY (CNH) account, then convert outside the mainland

Lawful where the offshore account is lawfully funded Offshore
Who can use it
An existing offshore account that can lawfully receive the funds; not a way around the mainland transfer rules.
Pros
Conversion then happens under the offshore jurisdiction's rules.
Cons
Does not solve the hard part — getting the money from the mainland account to the offshore account is itself the regulated step. The CNH spread is not published in the sources reviewed, so no price is shown.
PRC tax
The outbound leg is still governed by S05.
U.S. tax
§988 applies to the offshore disposal as well (S09).

M12 · Split the amount across relatives' quotas (分拆结售汇)

Prohibited Prohibited
Who can use it
None — this is the pattern the rules are written against.
Pros
None that survives review.
Cons
Art. 39 and art. 45 of S05 expose the amount to a fine of up to 30%, or 30%-100% where serious, plus confiscation of the gain. It is the single most expensive way to save a CNY 410 fee.
PRC tax
The fine is not a tax and is not deductible anywhere.
U.S. tax
No U.S. relief for a PRC penalty.

No — blank and flagged (F05)
