China foreign-exchange rules (official detail)

All quotes below are from official SAFE (State Administration of Foreign Exchange, China) publications. Links open the official pages.

1. Annual individual foreign-exchange quota — $50,000 per person per year

"The Detailed Rules specify that the annual quota for individual foreign exchange purchases be drastically raised from USD 20,000 to USD 50,000... Within the total annual quota, individuals can directly handle the formalities in the banks with their valid identity documents."

Source: SAFE, "SAFE Spokesman Addresses Press Conference on Relevant Issues Concerning the Detailed Rules on Measures for the Administration of Individual Foreign Exchange," Jan 5, 2007 — safe.gov.cn verified

2. Beyond the quota — documentation required

Foreign exchange sales/purchases beyond the quota require examination and verification of authenticity. For one-sided transfer items beyond the quota — donations, family maintenance remittances, inheritances — notarized agreements/contracts, direct-relative or notarized relationship certificates, and the overseas payer's income and tax-payment certificates (or inheritance legal/notarial documents) are required.

Source: SAFE, Jan 5, 2007 — safe.gov.cn verified

3. Cash rules (deposits, withdrawals, carrying cash abroad)

  • Depositing foreign cash: cumulative daily amount above USD 5,000 equivalent → handle at a bank with relevant documents.
  • Withdrawing foreign cash: cumulative daily amount above USD 10,000 equivalent → file related documents with SAFE in advance.
  • Carrying foreign cash abroad: daily cumulative amount above USD 10,000 equivalent → provide customs/bank declaration forms.

Source: SAFE, Jan 5, 2007 — safe.gov.cn verified

4. Overseas card withdrawals — RMB 10,000/day, RMB 100,000/year

Daily quota per card for overseas cash withdrawals with RMB cards and foreign currency cards is the equivalent of RMB 10,000. In case of overseas cash withdrawals in excess of the annual quota, the individual will not be allowed to withdraw cash overseas with domestic bank cards in the current and second years. Overseas consumption by cardholders does not offset the annual quota of USD 50,000 for foreign exchange purchases.

Source: SAFE, "Official ... Answers Media Questions on Regulating Large-sum Overseas Cash Withdrawals with Bank Cards," Dec 30, 2017 — safe.gov.cn verified

5. Foreigners working in China (salary repatriation)

Per reporting of the primary rules (PBOC [2006] No. 3 Measures for the Administration of Individual Foreign Exchange and SAFE [2007] No. 1 Detailed Rules): foreigners paid in RMB may convert their entire salary into foreign currency with an employment contract, work permit, and proof that Chinese individual income tax was paid. Practitioners state there is no annual quota for this channel, though banks may request additional documentation.

FLAGGED: this is a secondary source (Ecovis/BNA), not yet confirmed against the primary PBOC/SAFE text. Do not rely on it without checking with a Chinese bank or SAFE-authorized institution. See flagged items.

Source: Ecovis/BNA — ecovis.com secondary source

6. Penalties for circumvention (China side)

  • Individual FX transactions must be done through banks; illegal foreign-exchange transactions are prohibited.
  • The annual quota is not transferable; splitting quotas ("smurfing") is prohibited.
  • Illegal foreign-exchange trading above USD 200,000 equivalent may constitute a crime under PRC law.
  • SAFE maintains a national watchlist; listed individuals lose access to the annual quota for the listing year and the next two years (per HSBC China guide citing SAFE requirements).

Sources: Standard Chartered alert — sc.com secondary report of PRC rules; HSBC China guide — hsbc.com.cn secondary source; NCA report — nationalcrimeagency.gov.uk secondary source
