Why the ranking looks like this

  1. Cost is dominated by one line — the spread — and it is the same on every bank-conversion route. A different bank may beat 0.21%, but no lawful route removes it.
  2. The wire fee is capped. Above roughly USD 38,900 the marginal fee is zero, so splitting a large transfer into several wires only adds cable fees. The one exception is the resident convenience amount, which forces one wire per calendar year.
  3. Card and cash routes are capped by regulation, not by price. RMB 100,000 a year by ATM and USD 10,000 a trip by hand make them irrelevant at USD 400,000, whatever they cost per dollar.
  4. Documentation, not price, decides whether a route exists for you. W1 is cheapest; it only works if the bank accepts where the money came from.
