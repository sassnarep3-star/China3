The “conversion penalty”, line by line

People describe a “considerable tax” on getting money out of China. Decomposed against official sources, the Chinese side is a bank spread and a capped fee. The large number is a U.S. income tax on the currency gain — and it is avoidable only by basis and timing, not by choosing another rail.

The one real tax: IRC §988

The statute is short and does not care how you move the money. Disposing of foreign currency in a personal transaction, “no gain shall be recognized … The preceding sentence shall not apply if the gain which would otherwise be recognized on the transaction exceeds $200.” Above USD 200 of gain, the whole gain is ordinary income — not just the excess. IRC §988

Gain = amount received × (1 − today's rate ÷ your acquisition rate). With the 2026-11-10 ask of 6.6903 and the IRS 2024 yearly average of 7.189, that is 6.94% of everything you convert.

Ranking the routes by tax, not by fee

Every lawful route that disposes of CNY carries the same §988 gain, because the tax follows the currency and not the rail. What differs is the PRC tax attached to the underlying item, and the fine attached to a prohibited one.

What actually reduces the U.S. tax

  1. Get the basis right. Your basis is the USD value of the CNY on the day you acquired it. If part of the balance was acquired at a weaker yuan, that part carries a smaller gain. Sort the balance by acquisition date and rate before converting anything.
  2. Spread the conversion across tax years. The gain is ordinary income; keeping it inside a lower bracket in each year is the only lever the statute leaves you. Converting USD 400,000 at once can push you into the top bracket.
  3. Do not expect a loss to help. The quoted text exempts gain; it does not give a deduction for a personal loss. Treat a loss as non-deductible until an adviser says otherwise (flag C3-F15).
  4. Treat the reporting returns as the real penalty risk. FBAR at USD 10,000 aggregate and Form 8938 at USD 50,000/75,000 are information returns. Missing them costs more than the conversion does.
  5. Check whether the money is a gift or a bequest. A genuine gift from a foreign person above USD 100,000 is reported on Form 3520; that is a different analysis from selling your own currency.

What does not reduce the tax: using a different bank, an ATM, cash, Hong Kong, a fintech, or a relative. §988 follows the currency, not the rail. Prohibited routes add a fine on top.

If you have never filed an FBAR for this account

The exposure is the penalty table above, not the conversion cost. Prior-year non-filing is a separate question from how to move the money now, and it should be resolved with a tax adviser before you convert, because the transfer itself creates the record.
