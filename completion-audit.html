China money: independent completion audit

Official pages read on 10 November 2026. This is a supplemental audit, not a claim that every inherited line was independently reverified.

Executive summary: eligibility comes before price

First route to investigate: documented, lawful current-account income converted and wired by a regulated bank. https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2026/0713/3088.html specifically permits foreign individuals to purchase foreign exchange against valid identity and source-of-funds material showing the amount, including tax documentation. This supports a documented-income route; it does not establish that every balance or every US citizen qualifies.

https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2026/0713/3088.html: foreign individual remuneration. Its domestic-person FAQ also explains that the $50,000 annual convenience amount is not an absolute ceiling for qualifying documented transactions: official FAQ. Do not assume that domestic-person convenience rules apply to a foreign passport holder.

No exact least-tax winner is established. The source and ownership of the funds, prior tax treatment, currency basis, account classification and bank eligibility are not known. A cheaper transfer channel does not establish lower income tax. https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-currency-and-currency-exchange-rates requires relevant income and expense items to be translated into USD using the applicable timing rules: https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-currency-and-currency-exchange-rates currency guidance. This page leaves personal tax and all-in totals blank.

Practical bank workflow

  1. Identify the legal owner and source of each portion of the balance. Do not treat business, inheritance, gifts, property proceeds and wages as interchangeable.
  2. For the foreign-individual current-income route, assemble valid identity, evidence of the source and transaction amount, and tax documentation. The exact additional checklist remains bank-specific.
  3. Submit the genuine transaction to the bank for eligibility review; ask the bank to identify the actual obstacle and any claimed tax by legal basis.
  4. Compare the executable USD selling rate and itemized transfer fees, including correspondent and receiving fees, before authorizing conversion.
  5. After approval, convert and wire to the correctly identified recipient. Retain the rate confirmation, source evidence, tax evidence and wire receipt.

Steps 1 and 3–5 are practical recommendations, not assertions of a universal mandatory procedure. The official document requirement is https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2026/0713/3088.html article 59 explanation; published tariff exceptions and actual-rate disclaimer are BOC tariff and BOC board. No funds were moved and no account-specific approval is represented.

$10,000–$400,000: freshly sourced fee-only illustration

BOC USD board displayed 668.51 CNY per US$100 selling, 665.71 buying, timestamp 2026/11/10 17:39:58 (as displayed). Thus the illustrative selling rate is 6.6851 CNY/USD. This is a snapshot, not an executable quote. Source: BOC.

Published personal e-banking cross-border wire tariff: 0.1% of amount, ¥50 minimum / ¥260 maximum, plus ¥150 cable outside Hong Kong/Macao/Taiwan. BOC expressly allows agreed, promotional and local variations. Formula: min(max(USD × 6.6851 × 0.001, 50), 260) + 150. USD fee equivalents divide by 6.6851; these are derived illustrations, not officially quoted USD charges. Eligibility for e-banking at these sizes is not established. Official tariff.

USD amount means gross currency purchased, not net dollars received. Fees are modeled as additional CNY.
