What it actually costs to turn CNY into USD

The answer first. For a U.S. citizen converting a lawful RMB balance, the cost of conversion and transfer is small and it falls as the balance grows, because the only ad-valorem charge is capped. The money at risk is not in the conversion at all — it is in the tax treatment of whatever created the RMB, and in U.S. information-reporting penalties.

The single most important number. Moving USD 400,000 lawfully costs about $901.09 in exchange margin and bank charges — 0.225%. A single willful FBAR violation on the same balance carries a statutory maximum of $200,000.00. Reporting compliance is roughly 222× more expensive than the entire conversion. Fix the reporting first; the conversion is the cheap part.

Three honest limits on this model.

1. The FX margin is one bank, one morning. Retail pricing was deregulated and the PBC no longer caps the spread, so no official percentage exists for "the" CNY/USD spread. 0.20996 % is what Bank of China's own two published numbers implied on 2026-11-09. Your bank may be better or worse; the BOC page itself warns that the board is indicative and the counter rate governs.

2. The bank tariff is BOC's schedule. Other banks publish their own. The structure — ad valorem with a low cap, plus a flat cable fee — is what makes the cost regressive, and that structure is common, but the constants are BOC's.

3. Tax is not in these totals. PRC IIT on whatever created the RMB and U.S. § 988 gain on the currency movement are excluded because they depend on your acquisition history, which was not provided. They are quantified by rate in section 4 and on the penalty page, never guessed as a dollar figure.
