Getting money out of China, priced.

A U.S. citizen, a mainland China bank account, and a question that turns out to have a much smaller number in it than expected: converting USD 400,000 costs about $1,663 all-in. The risk that people describe as “a considerable amount of taxes” is real, but it is not a conversion charge — it is a U.S. information-reporting penalty that starts at $165,353 and can reach half the balance.

The three things that are not true.
There is no Chinese tax on converting RMB to USD. The USD 50,000 figure is a documentation threshold, not a tax and not a cap. And moving your own after-tax money to the U.S. is not a U.S. income event. Each of those has a source on the sources page, and each of them is what the words “conversion penalty” usually turn out to mean.

What this site will not do

It will not tell you a single number for your own situation, because the amount, the source of the money, your residence status, whether the account has ever been declared and whether prior filings exist are all unknown. Every figure here is conditional. It will not fill in a cost that no official page states — those cells are blank and listed on the flagged page. And it will not recommend a route that requires a false declaration, a borrowed quota or an unlicensed exchanger, however common those are.
