Most important finding. On the lawful bank route, the published bank costs are small — about 0.21% spread plus at most ¥410 per wire (intermediary and U.S. receiving fees are extra and unverified). The cost that can be large is U.S. tax on currency gain: the CNY has strengthened from an IRS yearly average of 7.189 per USD (2024) to a BOC selling rate of 6.6919 (2026-11-09). If your CNY was acquired near 2024 rates, converting it realises a currency gain of roughly 6.9% of the dollars received, and 26 U.S.C. 988 treats disposing of a foreign-currency bank deposit as a currency transaction. Whether the gain is excluded (personal transaction, gain ≤ $200), capital, or ordinary income depends on facts that were not supplied — so the tax amount is left blank. China has no tax on the conversion itself in any official source reviewed (see IR-011 on Flagged for Review).

How the tax law applies (what is verified, what is not)
  • Verified: disposing of nonfunctional currency, including "demand or time deposits … issued by a bank", is a section 988 transaction — 26 U.S.C. 988(c)(1)(C).
  • Verified: for an individual's personal transaction, gain is not recognised unless it exceeds $200 on the transaction — 988(e)(2).
  • Verified: a transaction is not "personal" to the extent its expenses qualify under section 162 (business) or 212 (investment) — 988(e)(3); section 988 gain is ordinary income — 988(a)(1)(A).
  • Unverified (blank): whether CNY savings in a Chinese deposit account are "personal" or section-212 holdings for this taxpayer; the resulting character (capital vs. ordinary); the tax rate; whether a loss (2021 row) is deductible. These need a U.S. tax professional and the actual acquisition dates.
