C. Chinese tax

What China charges on the money itself, as distinct from on moving it.

C01Is there a Chinese tax charged on converting RMB into USD?AnsweredNo official page read imposes a tax on the conversion itself. The Foreign Exchange Regulation prohibits evading foreign exchange and using false documents, and requires a genuine transaction basis; what the bank is required to see is the source of the funds and the tax evidence for them.Nothing - the official page settles it.If a bank or agent has quoted a 'conversion tax', ask for it in writing with the rule it relies on, and treat the figure as unconfirmed until then.S17 State Council S04 SAFE Open item U02

C03Is interest on a Chinese bank deposit taxed?AnsweredSavings-deposit interest has been exempt from individual income tax since 9 October 2008.Nothing - the official page settles it.Nothing to find. Interest earned after that date should not be the item the bank is questioning.S26 STA
C04What is charged on the sale of a home?AnsweredProperty-transfer income is taxed at 20% of the gain, the gain being the proceeds less the original value and reasonable costs.Your purchase price and deductible costs; other property taxes on the sale (VAT, deed tax, land appreciation tax) were not verified in this pass.Ask the property tax office for the settlement statement of the sale; it states the tax actually paid on that transaction and is the document the bank will want.S25 STA Open item U17

D01Is wiring your own money to the United States taxable income?AnsweredMoving your own funds is not itself income, but a disposition of foreign currency held as a bank deposit is a foreign-currency transaction whose gain or loss is ordinary income or loss; a U.S. citizen is taxed on worldwide income either way.Nothing - the official page settles it.Nothing to find. How to close the open items

In this order. Each document is useful on its own, and the first three decide whether the rest matters.

  1. 1. Statements by currency, 24 months — Shows every deposit, its size and whether any sub-account already holds foreign currency. Closes: A01, A02, A05
  2. 2. Written confirmation of your account category — Settles whether the bank treats you as a 境外个人 account; decides which rule your branch will apply. Closes: A02
  3. 3. Chinese tax payment record and withholding statements — Shows whether tax evidence exists and in what form; the bank will not proceed without it. Closes: A03, C08
  4. 4. Exchange slips and payroll or sale records with dates and rates — Fixes your dollar basis, and with it the U.S. currency-gain figure. Closes: A04, D02, D03
  5. 5. Property registry extract and the sale's tax settlement — Opens the bank-handled property route and sets the ceiling on what may be remitted. Closes: B03, C04, C05
  6. 6. Hukou cancellation certificate or notarised inheritance document — Decides whether a SAFE-approved property transfer is available at all. Closes: B04, B05
  7. 7. Your U.S. bank's inbound wire fee, and the Chinese bank's charge code — Prices the half of the cost that no official page publishes. Closes: E03
  8. 8. The written basis of the 'considerable tax' you were quoted — Identifies which of the three separate costs the figure refers to, or shows it is unfounded. Closes: D09, C01

What to put to the bank in writing

Ask these at the branch that holds the account, and keep the written answer. A branch cannot bind another branch, and an answer that contradicts the rules above should be checked against the official page it cites.

  • Is this account recorded as a 境外个人 (foreign individual) account, and under which document?
  • Which of these will you accept as tax evidence for this balance: tax payment certificate, exemption certificate, or the outbound-payment filing form?
  • What is your selling rate and all fees for this transfer today, in writing?
  • Will you remit this as cross-border RMB instead of buying USD here, and at what cost?
  • Which charge option will you use on the wire (shared or sender pays)?

The order that follows from the register

  1. Classify the money (A01-A05). Until the origin is known, every route is a guess.
  2. Fix the Chinese tax position (C01-C08). The bank will not move money whose tax evidence is missing, and the tax is owed whether or not it moves.
  3. Fix the U.S. reporting position (D04-D07). The penalties for a missed report are larger than the bank's fee by orders of magnitude, and they do not depend on which Chinese route you use.
  4. Then choose the route (B01-B08) and price it (E01-E06). Route choice changes the bank cost by tenths of a percent; it does not change the Chinese income tax or the U.S. reports.
