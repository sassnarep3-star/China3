Where the tax and the penalties actually are

No official source read in this pass taxes the conversion or the wire. The money at stake sits in four other places: Chinese tax on the income behind the balance, the bank's spread and fee, a U.S. currency gain, and U.S. reporting penalties.

The brief mentions "a considerable amount of taxes" but names no tax. Nothing below confirms what you were quoted. See U02.

Example: RMB acquired at the 2024 average and sold today for $400,000 shows a gain of $27,703. Whether the personal-transaction rule (no gain recognised up to $200) applies is a legal judgement: U05.

How to read this

  • If the tax was paid when the money was earned, the remaining cost of the transfer is the bank's: about a quarter to half of one percent.
  • If Chinese tax was never paid, that is the large number, and it is owed whether or not the money moves. The bank will not accept the transfer without tax evidence.
  • On the U.S. side, income belongs on the return for the year it was earned, with credit for Chinese tax paid. The transfer adds a possible currency gain and the reporting forms.
  • The prohibited routes cost far more than any tax here: fines start at up to 30% of the amount.
