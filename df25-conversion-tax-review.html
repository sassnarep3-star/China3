The U.S. tax on turning RMB into dollars

For a U.S. citizen with RMB in a Chinese bank. This page answers one question: what does the United States tax at the moment the RMB becomes dollars, and how much is it at each $10,000 step up to $400,000? It was read from the statute and the regulation, paragraph by paragraph, on 11 November 2026. Chinese tax, the bank's charges and the reporting forms are on the dashboard's Tax and Costs pages.

The answer

  • Only the currency gain is taxed. Sending home money you already own is not income again. The gain is the dollars you receive, minus what the same RMB cost you in dollars on the day you got it. 26 CFR 1.988-2 · 26 U.S.C. 1001
  • How big it is depends on when you got the RMB. At the Bank of China selling rate of 668.86 per $100 (11 November 2026, 15:30:04 Beijing), RMB acquired at the 2025 average shows a gain of +6.94% of the dollars received and at the 2024 average +7.05%; RMB acquired in October 2026 shows −0.06%; RMB acquired at the 2021 average shows a loss of 3.69%. BOC board · Fed annual series · Fed monthly series
  • Two boxes set the rate. Investment or business money: the gain is ordinary income (10% to 37%) and a loss is an ordinary loss. Personal money: a gain of $200 or less is ignored, a larger gain is reported as a capital gain (0%, 15% or 20% if held over a year), and a loss is not deductible. 26 U.S.C. 988 · IRS Pub. 525 · 26 U.S.C. 165 · IRS Topic 409
  • The official texts give the test for the box, not the answer for a savings balance. That is the main open item: DF25-F02.

Nothing here is a finding about any person. The averages stand in for dates nobody has supplied, and this page reports official texts and arithmetic; it is not advice on a return.
