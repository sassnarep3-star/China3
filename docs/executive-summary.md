# Executive Summary — Getting Money Out of China (CNY → USD) for a U.S. Citizen

**Project:** China3 — collect, organize, and analyze options for converting CNY held in a Chinese bank account into USD, with minimum tax and penalty exposure.
**Scope:** A U.S. citizen with money sitting in a Chinese bank account, no manual input required; research based on official/verified sources only.
**Research:** `research/cny-usd-conversion-research.md` (task T1). Every claim links to an official source or is flagged unverified.

---

## 1. The short answer

There is **no tax or penalty for simply converting your own CNY into USD and moving it out of China.** Moving your own money between accounts is not income. The real costs are:

1. **China's foreign-exchange rules** — annual quotas and documentation (SAFE/PBOC).
2. **Bank fees and foreign-exchange spreads** — amounts vary by bank (not fixed by regulation; left blank/unverified where not confirmed by an official source).
3. **U.S. reporting obligations** — FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) and possibly Form 8938 (FATCA). These are *information reports*, not taxes — but non-compliance carries severe penalties.
4. **U.S. income tax** — only on the *income that produced the money* if it was never taxed (worldwide taxation), plus any foreign-currency gain above $200 per conversion transaction (IRC §988 personal-transaction rule).

**Bottom line:** use a legal bank channel, stay within documentation rules, and file your U.S. reports. That is dramatically cheaper than any penalty.

## 2. Best route by source of the money

| Source of CNY in China | Recommended legal route | China limit | U.S. tax consequence of the move itself |
|---|---|---|---|
| Post-tax salary earned while working in China | Salary repatriation via Chinese bank with employment contract, work permit, and Chinese tax-payment proof | No annual cap (documentation-based) — *secondary source, flagged* | Not taxable; FBAR reporting if aggregate foreign accounts >$10,000 |
| Savings or other funds, up to $50,000/yr | Bank FX purchase (CNY→USD) + international wire | $50,000 USD-equivalent per person per year; valid ID within quota | Not taxable; possible §988 FX gain if gain >$200 on the transaction |
| Savings or other funds, above $50,000/yr | Bank FX purchase with supporting evidence (emigration, medical, tuition, family maintenance, inheritance) through a SAFE-authorized bank | Above quota allowed only with documentation | Same as above |
| Any amount, small | Carrying USD cash across the border | Declare >$5,000; permission >$10,000 | Not taxable; FBAR still applies to the account |

**Avoid entirely:** underground banking ("huayi"/"duiqiao"), "smurfing" (splitting quotas through friends/family), crypto P2P, falsified trade invoices, undeclared cash >$10,000. Risks: fines, multi-year foreign-exchange bans, frozen accounts, and — for illegal FX trading above $200,000 — criminal liability.

## 4. What is NOT a penalty (myths cleared)

- **There is no "conversion tax"** in China on changing CNY to USD.
- **There is no U.S. tax on moving your own money** between accounts.
- **FBAR and Form 8938 are not taxes** — they are disclosure forms. The penalty comes from *not filing*, not from the money itself.
- **The $50,000 China quota is not a tax** — it's a compliance limit; documented cases (salary with tax proof, emigration, medical, tuition, inheritance) can exceed it.

---

## 5. Recommended action checklist (no manual research needed by you)

1. **Identify the source** of the CNY (salary vs. savings vs. gift/inheritance).
2. **Choose the legal channel:** salary repatriation (if salary) or bank FX purchase + wire (up to $50,000/yr; documented legitimate purpose above that).
3. **Keep documentation:** exchange receipts, wire confirmations, tax-payment certificates, employment contract/work permit (if salary).
4. **File U.S. reports:** FBAR via BSA E-Filing (bsaefiling.fincen.gov) if aggregate foreign accounts exceeded $10,000 at any point; Form 8938 if thresholds met.
5. **Claim U.S. relief:** FEIE (Form 2555) and/or Foreign Tax Credit (Form 1116) on any Chinese-taxed income.
6. **Do not use underground banking, crypto P2P, or quota-splitting.**
