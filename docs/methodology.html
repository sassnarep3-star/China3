Method

How verification was done and what it does not cover.

What was done

Every factual claim on this site was traced to a primary document — a statute, a State Council regulation, a People's Bank of China or SAFE measure, a tax authority publication, a Treasury or IRS page, a treaty text, or an eCFR/CFR provision — and the specific proposition was read on the page itself. The register at Sources records, for each URL, what it supports and the date it was checked (2026-11-07).

The three tiers

TierMeaningHow it is used
officialIssued by the authority itself: statute, regulation, treaty, agency page, or the official gazette.Carries every number and every legal proposition.
official-secondaryPublished by the same authority but explanatory: a Q&A, an operations guide, a consolidated index of regulations in force.Used to pin down practical detail — who qualifies, what documents, what is still in force.
third-partyA non-government reproduction of a legal text.Used only where the official site could not be reached, and always flagged on Flagged for review.

What "blank and flagged" means

If a value could not be confirmed from an official source it was not filled with a plausible number, an industry estimate, or a memory. It was left blank, marked unverified, and written up on the flagged page with the reason. The clearest example is retail FX cost: no regulator publishes retail CNY/USD spreads, so the cost model shows the formula and a calculator and ships with no rates at all.

Two of the twenty-seven sources are third-party reproductions of the United States Code (§ 988 and § 5321). The statutes' content is not in doubt; the URLs are, which is why they are flagged.

What this research deliberately does not cover

  • Your facts. Four determinations depend entirely on documents only you hold: your FX category, whether a cancelled household registration exists, when the balance was acquired relative to your emigration, and what created the money. Each is itemised on the flagged page.
  • Your bank's prices. Spreads, wire fees and intermediary deductions are commercial, not regulatory, and were not estimated.
  • Corporate structures. If the money sits inside a Chinese company rather than your personal account, a different framework applies (profit distribution, dividend withholding, and separate US forms). Not addressed here.
  • Renunciation and the US expatriation tax. Mentioned only as a thing not to do casually; not researched.
  • State tax. Only federal US obligations are described.

Reproducing the verification

The data is machine-checked, not just proofread. Three scripts do the work:

ScriptWhat it enforces
scripts/validate_data.pyUnique IDs; every source reference resolves; a stated cap must be marked verified; an unverified item must carry a flag; every consequence must have a source.
scripts/check_links.pyEvery internal link and anchor resolves; every external URL in the register is well formed and de-duplicated; source chips on each page point at a real register entry.
scripts/build_site.pyRefuses to build if the contract check fails, then inlines the data and generates every page.

Run python3 scripts/validate_data.py && python3 scripts/check_links.py && python3 scripts/build_site.py.

Currency of the rules

Two things changed recently and both are reflected here. Chinese crypto regulation was replaced on 6 February 2026 by 银发〔2026〕42号, which repealed the 2021 notice that most articles still cite. And an index published by SAFE as at 30 June 2026 confirms that the 2008 FX Regulations, the 2006 individual FX measures, the 2007 implementing rules and the 2003 cash-carrying rules were still in force, which is why they are relied on here rather than assumed.
