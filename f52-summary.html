The answer in five points

  1. Converting and wiring is not taxed. No official source read puts a tax on the conversion or the wire; the State does not restrict recurring international payments and transfers. What the bank must see is a genuine lawful basis plus source/tax documents. F-S08 F-S04
  2. The large number, if real, is tax on the underlying income — wages/comprehensive income at 3%–45%, or 20% on dividends, rent and property gains — owed whether or not the money moves, and only if it was never paid. Bank-deposit interest is not in the nine taxable categories as fetched; its exemption notice was not re-fetched today (flagged via F52-U11 practice: ask the tax office). F-S07
  3. The bank's own cost is about two-tenths of one percent. $53.42 on $10,000 and $901.20 on $400,000 in one wire at BOC today. Intermediary and U.S. receiving fees are unpublished — blank, so these are lower bounds. F-S01 F-S02 F52-U04
  4. A U.S. passport does not buy the $50,000 ID-only amount. Only Chinese citizens and Foreign Permanent Resident ID holders do. On a passport, the standard route is documented lawful income (F01); home-sale, inheritance, emigrant-property and change-back routes each fit one kind of money. F-S05 F-S04 F-S09
  5. The U.S. side can exceed the bank cost. Selling RMB is a taxable currency disposition (gain illustrated up to $27,703 on $400,000 if acquired at the 2024 IRS average — gain, not tax), and missed FBAR/Form 8938/Form 3520 filings carry penalties up to $16,536 / $60,000 / 25% respectively.
