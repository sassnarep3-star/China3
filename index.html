What it actually costs to get your money out of China

A U.S. citizen with CNY in a mainland bank account. Every method priced from $10,000 to $400,000 in $10,000 steps, every number traced to an official page, every unknown left blank and flagged.

The short answer. No official source imposes a Chinese tax on converting your own savings; the PRC income-tax law taxes income, not the exchange. What you pay in China is a bank spread of 0.21%, a wire fee capped at CNY 260, and a CNY 150 cable fee. The large number is on the U.S. side: if you acquired the CNY at the 2024 average rate, converting produces an IRC §988 currency gain of about 6.94% of the dollars you receive, taxed as ordinary income — roughly 1.67% of the amount at a 24% marginal rate. That is the “considerable tax”, and no bank, ATM or Hong Kong rail avoids it.

Do this first — four facts decide everything

  1. Whose money is it, and where did it come from? Salary, inheritance, property sale, company dividend, or untraceable savings. This single answer selects the route.
  2. Are you 境内个人 or 境外个人? A Chinese citizen, or a foreigner who has resided in China continuously for one year, is 境内个人. That decides whether the USD 50,000 convenience amount even applies to you.
  3. When did you acquire the CNY, and at what rate? That sets your U.S. §988 basis and therefore the only large tax number in this whole exercise.
  4. What has already been filed? FBAR and Form 8938 are information returns with penalties that dwarf the conversion cost.

Only the last two are about tax. Neither is a Chinese charge.

What not to do

Splitting money through relatives' quotas, underground banks, and stablecoin conversions are all prohibited, and each carries a statutory fine of up to 30% — 30% to 100% if serious — before any criminal exposure. See the prohibited routes.
