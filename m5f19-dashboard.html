The headline answer, before anything else. For a U.S. citizen, the money does not move because of a tax. It moves because of documents and because of which category the bank recorded you in. The published cost of a documented transfer is USD 53.38 on USD 10,000 and USD 899.01 on USD 400,000 — bank spread plus one wire fee plus one telecom charge. The largest number in this whole subject is not an exchange rate at all: it is a U.S. reporting penalty of up to 50% of the account balance for a willful FBAR failure, which is USD 200,000 on a USD 400,000 balance.
What this edition corrects. Earlier layers of this repository tell a U.S. citizen that a personal wire needs no approval “up to USD 50,000 per day on ID” and cite a SAFE page whose own question is about a domestic individual (境内个人). The article that actually governs a passport-holder remitting foreign currency out of an FX account is Article 65(1) of the 2020 Current-Account Guidelines, and it states no amount threshold at all — identity document only. Article 65 appears nowhere else in this repository. See Which rules apply and flag G01 on Flagged for review.

Tax on the act of converting

no such tax found

Currency conversion is not among the nine closed categories of taxable personal income in PRC IIT Law Art. 2. This says nothing about tax on the income that created the money.

Willful FBAR penalty, per violation

USD 200,000

Greater of USD 165,353 (inflation-adjusted) or 50% of the balance in the account at the time of the violation. On USD 400,000 the 50% limb governs. A per-violation maximum, not a ceiling on total exposure.

www.govinfo.gov ↗ F47,F48

FBAR filing trigger

aggregate > USD 10,000

Aggregate across all foreign accounts, at any time during the calendar year. Due 15 April, automatic extension to 15 October, e-filed with FinCEN and not with the return.

www.irs.gov ↗ F45,F46

U.S. tax on the currency gain

USD 0 if personal

26 U.S.C. §988(e) excludes gain on a personal disposition of nonfunctional currency — unless the gain exceeds USD 200, or the transaction is not personal under §988(e)(3). Your facts are unknown, so this is conditional.

What is genuinely unknown

The brief supplies no amount, no account documents, no source-of-funds history, no residence history, no bank, no receiving bank and no U.S. filing history. Those facts decide the route, the tax and the total. Everything here is therefore conditional, and the cells that depend on those facts are blank rather than estimated. Intermediary and beneficiary-bank charges, the executable spread, the offshore CNY basis, the Form 8938 threshold, U.S.–China treaty relief and state tax are all blank on Tax & penalties.
