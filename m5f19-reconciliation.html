Why they differ, in full

  1. The board moved during the day. Sessions fbbd3b22, d6467425 and 775c9619 all read the BOC board at 07:32:39 on 2026-11-10 and used ask 6.6903 / bid 6.6622. This session read it at 17:39:58 and got ask 6.6851 / bid 6.6571. The board is a live page, so the same code run at a different hour produces different dollars. This is a property of the source, not an error in anyone’s arithmetic.
  2. Two spread denominators. Dividing the half-spread by the ask gives 0.209421% at today’s board; dividing by the midpoint gives 0.209860%. On the legacy board the same choice produced 0.210005% versus 0.210446%, which is exactly the USD 0.05 gap between USD 53.42 and USD 53.47 at USD 10,000. Neither is wrong; they measure against different baselines and must never be mixed in one column.
  3. A wire count. The USD 400,000 gap between USD 901.30 and USD 1,332.05 is not a rate difference at all. It is one wire versus eight. The spread is proportional so it does not change; the CNY 260 fee ceiling and the flat CNY 150 telecom charge are per transaction, so eight wires pay the fixed element eight times.
  4. And the eight-wire premise itself does not survive the text. Guidelines Art. 65(1) states no amount threshold for an overseas individual remitting FX from an FX account, and Art. 64(1) lets a domestic individual exceed USD 50,000 in a single day on documents. SAFE Ningxia states there is no amount limit on genuine, lawful current-account purchase and settlement. Nothing in any of these requires eight transfers over eight years. The scenario is legitimate as a contingency if a bank stages the transfer, but it must not be presented as what the regulation requires.

Which number should you use?

Use USD 899.01 on USD 400,000 and USD 53.38 on USD 10,000 — one wire, spread against the ask, on the 2026-11-10 17:39:58 board — and treat both as models. Then get a written bank quotation, which is the only thing that closes the gap between a model and a price. The earlier figures are not wrong; they answer a slightly different question, and now the difference is documented instead of argued about.
