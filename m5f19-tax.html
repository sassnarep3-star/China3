The conversion penalty, decomposed

What people call the “conversion penalty” is four different things that are routinely added together. Separated, only one of them is a recurring cost of moving the money.

Worked example on a USD 400,000 balance. Reported correctly and moved on documents: the known China-side cost is USD 899.01 (0.002247535564165083544000837684%), plus whatever PRC tax was already due on the income, plus U.S. tax only if §988(e) does not exclude the gain. Not reported, and treated as willful: USD 200,000, being 50% of the balance, which exceeds the USD 165,353 adjusted fixed maximum — and that is a per-violation figure, since 31 CFR 1010.821(b) provides that the adjusted maxima do not limit the total where a penalty may be imposed for each day a violation continues. The reporting decision is worth roughly 220 times the exchange decision.
