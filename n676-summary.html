The five things that matter

1. Converting and wiring is not what gets taxed

Decree 532 art. 5 does not restrict current-account payments and transfers, and no official page read here taxes the conversion or the wire. What the bank must see is where the money came from and that Chinese tax on it was dealt with.

N06 State Council (via SAFE) N05 SAFE Tianjin branch

2. The real tax, if there is one, is on the income underneath

If Chinese individual income tax was never paid, it is owed whether or not the money moves: 3% to 45% on comprehensive income, 20% flat on interest, dividends, rent and property gains. That is orders of magnitude larger than the bank cost.

N09 State Taxation Administration N09b State Taxation Administration

3. The bank's own cost is a fifth of one percent or less

The rate cost is 0.20998% of every dollar, and the wire fee is 1 per mille capped at RMB 260 plus a RMB 150 telegraph charge. Above about $38,857 the fee is pinned at the cap, so one wire is always cheapest.

N01 Bank of China N02 Bank of China

4. A U.S. passport does not get the USD 50,000 allowance

The annual convenience amount applies only to holders of a Chinese Foreign Permanent Resident ID. A 境外个人 buys dollars on ID plus source-of-funds documents including tax certificates. And the amount is not a cap: above it you simply cannot proceed on ID alone.

N03 SAFE Tianjin branch N04 SAFE Ningxia branch

5. The U.S. side can be the bigger number

Selling RMB is a section 988 transaction and the gain is ordinary income. The personal-transaction relief disappears the moment the gain exceeds $200, which at $10,000 it does. Separately, a missed FBAR carries up to $16,536 non-willful and $165,353 willful, per violation.

N11 U.S. Government Publishing Office N12 FinCEN / eCFR

Do not do these

Splitting the quota across relatives or friends

SAFE treats it as evasion. The people involved go on a watch list for the year of listing and the following two years, during which every conversion needs transaction documents. Fines run to 30% of the amount, 30-100% in serious cases, with criminal liability possible.

N06 State Council (via SAFE)

Private swaps, money changers, false invoices, crypto

Art. 45 covers private buying and selling, disguised dealing, reselling and illegally brokering FX: confiscation of illegal gains, fines up to 30%, 30-100% in serious cases, criminal liability. Art. 40 covers buying FX on false documents.
