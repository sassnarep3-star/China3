Primary rules for a U.S. passport holder with RMB in a Chinese bank

What SAFE, PBOC and the State Council actually say about converting and remitting RMB as a non-domestic individual (境外个人), with the original Chinese text, a working translation and an official link on every row.

Key finding. The route for a foreign national's China-earned RMB income is Art. 59 of SAFE's Current-Account Guidelines (2020 ed.): valid ID plus source-of-funds materials showing the transaction amount, including tax certificates. It states no annual amount; only a holder of a Foreigner's Permanent Residence ID Card is subject to the USD 50,000 annual convenience amount. SAFE Tianjin quoted this Article again on 2026-07-13.

Analysis (an inference, not a quoted rule). If a "tax" cost appeared at the counter, the tax-certificate requirement in Art. 59 is a plausible cause: the bank wants proof that PRC tax on the income was dealt with before it converts. Whether any tax is actually owed depends on the type of income and its treatment (see U13 and U14 below).

Read before relying. Banks may refuse and must give a reason and an appeal channel (Art. 68). The Guidelines were amended in 2023 and 2024 (SAFE catalogue); the text here is the 2020 version, Art. 59 unchanged. Whether a U.S. citizen who has lived in China for a year or more is a domestic or non-domestic individual is unresolved in the texts (R01, VB-06). General information, not advice.

R04The USD 50,000 'convenience amount'
Individuals
USD 50,000 per person per year for individuals' FX sales and domestic individuals' FX purchases. It is not a limit: above it, ID alone is not enough and transaction-amount materials are needed.Guidelines Art. 54; SAFE Ningxia Q&A (2022)
SAFE Current-Account Guidelines 2020 (汇发〔2020〕14号) - gov.cn · Art. 54 (the USD 50,000 convenience amount) · Tier 1
Quoted text [E01.h]
个人经常项目外汇业务应具有真实、合法的交易背景。个人结汇和境内个人购汇实行年度便利化额度管理，便利化额度分别为每人每年等值5万美元。
Working translation: Individual current-account FX business must have a genuine and lawful transaction background. Individuals' FX sales and domestic individuals' FX purchases are subject to an annual convenience amount, USD 50,000 equivalent per person per year for each.

Working translation: The annual convenience amount is not a limit on an individual's yearly FX purchases or sales; it only means purchases or sales above USD 50,000 equivalent cannot be handled on the person's valid ID alone.
