# A502 independent accuracy audit — 2026-11-10

Scope: targeted review of `research/s775-executive-summary.md`, `s775-dashboard.html`, the first line of `data/s775_cost_10k_400k.csv`, and the cited primary sources. This is **not** a line-by-line verification of the entire repository, a legal opinion, a current rate quote, or a determination of this user's eligibility. Passing automated validators cannot establish the substance of sources, currency-flow consistency, or completeness of a tax analysis.

## Actionable findings

1. **Scope of $50,000 rules (high).** The S775 dashboard calls a personal wire “lawful - unrestricted” and claims “no approval up to US$50,000/day on ID.” Its own cited [SAFE Tianjin 2024 article](https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2024/0229/2402.html) is about *domestic individuals* remitting existing foreign currency for current-account expenditure: at or below the daily threshold ID is required, and above it amount-bearing documents are also required; purpose consistency review applies. It is not a blanket rule for a US citizen buying USD with CNY. For an overseas individual with legitimate current-account RMB income, [SAFE Tianjin 2026](https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2026/0713/3088.html) specifies ID and source-of-funds documents with amounts including tax documentation. [SAFE Tianjin 2025](https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2025/0715/2834.html) provides that tax documents need not be provided where the income is legally not taxable. Classification and requirements need confirmation. Annual convenience and daily remittance rules are distinct.
2. **CNY input and net USD conflict (high).** The $10,000 S775 row reports CNY needed of 66,903, ask 6.6903 CNY/USD, and net $9,946.58. On the row's own assumptions 66,903 / 6.6903 = $10,000 before fees; deducting a mid-market spread from that USD amount again as well as the wire charge mixes benchmarks. Where the CNY wire charge is paid separately, the converted amount before intermediary fees is $10,000, not $9,946.58; if it is paid from the same CNY budget, the amount converted is lower. Both cases need explicit assumptions. The recorded benchmark is a model, not an independent official cost. The [BOC tariff](https://www.boc.cn/ebanking/service/cs1/200905/t20090506_990712.html) says personal e-banking cross-border wire: 1‰, minimum RMB 50, maximum RMB 260, plus RMB 150 telecom charge outside Hong Kong/Macao/Taiwan; its own notes allow agreement, discounts, and local differences. The [BOC rates page](https://www.boc.cn/sourcedb/whpj/) is a changing source and does not establish a transaction's executable quote.
3. **Unverified absolute tax assertion (high).** “There is no PRC tax on converting your own CNY to USD” is an unsupported universal conclusion: the cited [SAFE rules](https://www.safe.gov.cn/tianjin/2026/0713/3088.html) address documentation, not an exhaustive tax exemption for every source, transaction, person, and year. They do not establish that tax is payable on conversion either. Establish nature and tax history of funds and consult the responsible Chinese tax authority/adviser; do not invent an amount.
4. **$200 and §988 (medium).** The dashboard's categorical statement that personal transactions are excluded and gains ≤$200 are excluded needs the exact conditions: [26 USC §988(e)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2023-title26/html/USCODE-2023-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapN-partIII-subpartJ-sec988.htm) provides a personal-transaction rule and a de minimis gain rule where the transaction is personal and gain does not exceed $200, while §988(e)(3) excludes transactions to the extent expenses properly allocable to them meet specified §162 or §212 requirements. Section 988(c)(1)(C)(ii) includes qualifying bank deposits in nonfunctional currency. The $200 is not a general exemption for every foreign-account transaction. Currency character, basis, and application to the actual deposit remain unverified.
5. **Tax minimization cannot be ranked without facts (medium).** [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-tax-credit) says a foreign tax credit may be available on qualifying taxes, subject to restrictions, and foreign taxes on excluded income cannot be credited. [IRS FEIE](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-earned-income-exclusion) requires qualifying foreign earned income and residence/presence criteria. Neither provision establishes eligibility here; no claim that either is automatically optimal is justified.
6. **Reporting and remediation (medium).** [FinCEN's instructions](https://bsaefiling.fincen.gov/resources/FinCENFBARHelp.pdf) specify FBAR where aggregate foreign accounts exceed $10,000 at any time in the calendar year. Form 8938 differs; [IRS comparison](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/comparison-of-form-8938-and-fbar-requirements). A prior missed filing is not automatically eligible for streamlined procedures: [IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/streamlined-filing-compliance-procedures) specifies eligibility and non-willfulness. Do not treat a potential maximum penalty as the actual penalty or recommend a disclosure route without assessing facts.
7. **Verification limitations (medium).** `scripts/check_links.py` checks only `docs/*.html`, and external HTTP checks are optional. `scripts/verify_s775_site.py` checks domain membership and formula consistency, not truth or underlying rate provenance. A successfully linked source is not evidence it supports the corresponding row. The S775 dashboard's three cost rows have no link in each row. Repository-wide line-by-line checking is not established by this targeted audit.
