# R2 — U.S. citizen tax, currency and reporting obligations

**Research date:** 2026-11-07  
**Scope:** U.S. federal treatment of moving CNY held in China into USD in the United States. State/local tax, sanctions screening, bank-specific compliance and the user's complete filing history are not established.  
**Evidence rule:** “Verified” means the linked official text was opened and checked at the cited provision where the document parser made it available. It is not a personalized tax opinion. Unknown income years, tax basis, filing status, residency facts and penalties are left blank rather than estimated.

## 1. Controlling conclusions

| ID | Verified rule or scoped finding | Practical consequence | Official source |
|---|---|---|---|
| US-F01 | A U.S. citizen is generally subject to U.S. tax on worldwide income whether living in the United States or abroad. Benefits such as the foreign earned income exclusion (FEIE) and foreign tax credit (FTC) require a return and their own eligibility tests. | **Do not wait until repatriation to analyze tax.** Reconstruct wages, business income, interest, dividends, rent, gifts and asset disposals by the year in which each was recognized for U.S. purposes. | [IRS, U.S. citizens and resident aliens abroad](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/us-citizens-and-resident-aliens-abroad); [IRS international tax FAQ, General FAQ 1](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/frequently-asked-questions-about-international-individual-tax-matters) |
| US-F02 | The reviewed sources establish tax on income and on certain foreign-currency gain, not a separate federal “repatriation fee” for wiring one's existing bank balance to oneself. A same-owner transfer does not by itself prove that the balance was previously taxed or that no gain exists. | Separate three questions: (1) Was the original income reported? (2) Does converting/spending CNY realize currency gain? (3) Which account/asset reports apply? Never relabel an unexplained balance as “after-tax savings.” | [IRS worldwide-income guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/us-citizens-and-resident-aliens-abroad); [26 U.S.C. §988(a), (c)(1)(C) and (e)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-138/t26/s988/) |
| US-F03 | For a taxpayer whose functional currency is USD, disposing of nonfunctional currency—including a nonfunctional-currency bank deposit—is a §988 transaction. §988 gain/loss is generally ordinary unless another rule applies. For an individual's qualifying personal transaction, no gain is recognized if the otherwise recognized gain does not exceed USD 200; above that amount the personal exclusion does not apply. | Obtain CNY acquisition dates and USD basis by lot, not just the wire date. Determine whether each conversion/use is personal, business or investment. Do not automatically call all FX gain capital gain or assume every transfer is taxable. | [26 U.S.C. §988(c)(1)(C), (e)(1)–(3)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-138/t26/s988/); [IRS Publication 525, “Foreign currency transactions”](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p525) |
| US-F04 | U.S. returns report in USD. IRS says it has no single official exchange rate and generally accepts a posted rate used consistently; a single-day transaction uses that day's rate, while evenly received annual income may use a yearly average. FBAR instead uses each account's maximum local-currency value converted at the year-end Treasury rate. | Use different, documented rates for different purposes; do not reuse the bank's remittance quote as the FBAR or historical-income rate. | [IRS yearly-average exchange-rate guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/yearly-average-currency-exchange-rates); [IRS FBAR valuation and filing details](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/details-on-reporting-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts) |
| US-F05 | FEIE applies to qualifying foreign **earned** income with a foreign tax home and either bona-fide residence or the 330-full-day physical-presence test. Pensions/annuities are not earned income; dividends, interest and other non-service receipts are not made eligible merely because the taxpayer lives in China. | Test eligibility separately for every income year. A wire from China does not turn old savings, pensions, rent, dividends or gains into excludable foreign earned income. | [IRS FEIE eligibility](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-earned-income-exclusion); [IRS, what is foreign earned income](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-earned-income-exclusion-what-is-foreign-earned-income); [IRS Publication 54](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p54) |
| US-F06 | FTC generally covers a legal, actual foreign income tax paid/accrued by the taxpayer, subject to category, source and limitation rules. The allowable credit is limited to the smaller of qualifying foreign tax or U.S. tax attributable to the relevant foreign-source income. No credit/deduction is allowed for tax allocated to income excluded under FEIE/foreign-housing exclusions; refundable or non-owed amounts also fail. | Match Chinese tax receipts to the same taxpayer, year, income and FTC category. A Chinese tax receipt does not guarantee a dollar-for-dollar U.S. credit. | [IRS Topic 856, Foreign tax credit](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc856); [IRS qualifying foreign taxes](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-taxes-that-qualify-for-the-foreign-tax-credit) |
| US-F07 | The U.S.–China income-tax treaty and technical explanation contain source-country caps and double-tax coordination, but also a saving clause preserving U.S. taxation of U.S. citizens subject to specified exceptions. Treaty residence, beneficial ownership, income characterization and procedural relief cannot be inferred from citizenship. | Do not display “10% total tax” or “treaty eliminates U.S. tax.” Analyze the treaty article and saving-clause exception for each item before using a reduced Chinese rate or U.S. position. | [IRS-hosted U.S.–China treaty](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/china.pdf); [IRS-hosted technical explanation](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/chintech.pdf) |
| US-F08 | FBAR and Form 8938 are independent. FBAR can apply when aggregate foreign financial accounts exceed USD 10,000 at any time in the calendar year; Form 8938 uses higher asset thresholds and is attached to the income-tax return. Filing one does not replace the other. | Reconstruct every year's maximum balances before closing or moving accounts. A year-end zero balance does not erase the FBAR test, and Form 8938 also has an “any time during the year” limb. | [FinCEN FBAR filing rule](https://www.fincen.gov/report-foreign-bank-and-financial-accounts); [IRS Form 8938/FBAR comparison](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/comparison-of-form-8938-and-fbar-requirements) |
| US-F09 | A true foreign gift/bequest may be excluded from gross income yet trigger Form 3520. A transfer from a foreign corporation/partnership can be recharacterized, and foreign-trust distributions use a different part/rules. Calling a relative's transfer “my savings” does not resolve donor/recipient ownership. | Document the actual sender and legal event. Own-account principal, a donor's gift, an inheritance, a trust distribution and a corporate dividend are not interchangeable. | [IRS gifts from foreign persons](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/gifts-from-foreign-person); [IRS Form 3520 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i3520) |
| US-F10 | Physical transport of currency/monetary instruments over USD 10,000 at one time into or out of the United States requires FinCEN Form 105. The form expressly excludes a normal banking-procedure transfer that does not physically transport currency/monetary instruments. Structuring to evade U.S. reporting is prohibited. | A bank wire and hand-carried cash have different reports. Never divide cash, deposits or travelers among companions to defeat a threshold. | [FinCEN Form 105 and instructions](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/fin105_cmir.pdf); [31 U.S.C. §5324](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-138/t31/s5324/) |

| US-R07 | Purported gift from a foreign corporation or foreign partnership | Form 3520 threshold is USD 20,573 for 2026 (USD 20,116 for 2025), aggregated under the related-party rule. | US-R08 | Distribution from a Chinese company, partnership, trust or investment fund | Classify as dividend, salary, loan repayment, return of capital, trust distribution or fund income before choosing a form. A normal non-cash bank wire is not reported on this form. | Withdrawal/source records, exact instrument inventory, group-travel facts, Chinese exit documents, FinCEN 105 and CBP acknowledgment. | [FinCEN Form 105 instructions](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/fin105_cmir.pdf); [CBP currency-reporting guidance](https://www.cbp.gov/travel/us-citizens/know-before-you-go/know-you-go-traveling-abroad) |
