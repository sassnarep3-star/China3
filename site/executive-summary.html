Executive Summary — Getting Money Out of China Into USD, as a US Citizen

Prepared: 7 November 2026 · Scope: CNY held in a Chinese bank account, owned by a US citizen, to be converted to USD and made usable in the United States. Evidence base: 30+ official primary sources (SAFE, PBOC, State Council Gazette, STA, NPC, IRS, eCFR, FinCEN, CBP, Federal Reserve, Bank of China). Every figure in this repository links to one. Nothing is estimated.

1. The headline finding: the premise is wrong, and the error is expensive

The question assumes there is a large tax on converting CNY into USD. There is not.

What people believeWhat the primary sources say
China taxes you for taking money outNo PRC exit tax on individuals. The pre-departure obligation is to settle tax that is already due, not to pay a tax on leaving.
Converting CNY to USD triggers a big taxNo. Individual Income Tax Law Art. 2 lists every taxable event, and a currency conversion of your own funds is not one of them. Deposit interest would be the taxable item — and it has been exempt since 9 October 2008.
Inheriting from China means paying estate taxNo PRC estate tax exists. The Ministry of Finance has stated no estate-tax statute or draft has ever been published.
The US taxes the money when it arrivesNo US tax on a genuine gift or inheritance. The US imposes reporting, and the penalties are for not reporting.
So where does the "considerable tax" come from?See §3. It is almost always a penalty for a missed form, or an exchange-control fine for using a route that is not lawful. Both are avoidable.

The unavoidable cost of a lawful conversion is the bank's currency spread: about 0.42%. Read from Bank of China's published USD table on 7 November 2026 (buying 666.48, selling 669.29 per 100 USD — a 2.81 CNY spread).

2. The real constraint is permission, not tax

The binding problem is not what you owe. It is whether the State Administration of Foreign Exchange will let the transfer happen at all, and on what evidence.

  • Up to USD 50,000 per person per year, a bank will sell foreign currency on your ID alone. That is the 年度便利化额度 — a documentary convenience, not a legal ceiling.
  • Above that amount, the transaction is still lawful — SAFE's own published Q&A says there is no amount limit on genuine current-account conversion. But you must produce evidence of the underlying transaction.
  • Some purposes are closed. The FX purchase application names six prohibitions, including using purchased foreign currency for overseas property, overseas securities, and life or return-of-premium insurance. These are capital-account items China has not opened.
  • Two capital-account routes are open and designed for exactly this problem: the emigrant transfer (for someone who has given up mainland household registration and settled abroad; paid in three stages over two years) and the inheritance transfer (for a foreign-citizen heir). Both require a PRC tax payment certificate.
  • The three routes widely recommended online are prohibited and separately penalised: splitting the sum across relatives' quotas, underground-bank "offset" arrangements, and the USDT/crypto bridge. Since 2021 the last of these is classified outright as illegal financial activity, and banks are barred from processing it.

4. The recommendation, ranked by effective cost

  1. Use the ordinary bank route first. USD 50,000 per person per year on ID alone, at about 0.42%. If more than one family member genuinely owns part of the balance, each can use their own quota for their own money. That is lawful; moving your money through their quota is not.
  2. For anything above the quota, identify the real underlying event. Study costs, medical costs, wages, family support, service fees, or an actual trade. With evidence, the ceiling disappears.
  3. If the money is an inheritance, or if you have emigrated, use the dedicated capital-account route. These are the only routes designed to move a whole balance, and they cost administrative effort rather than tax.
  4. File every US form, every year, before doing anything else. FBAR (FinCEN 114) above USD 10,000 aggregate; Form 8938 above the applicable threshold; Form 3520 Part IV above USD 100,000 from a foreign individual. These obligations attach to the account you already have, not to the transfer.
  5. If filings were missed, correct them deliberately. The Streamlined Foreign Offshore Procedures eliminate the FBAR and information-return penalties entirely (0% offshore penalty) for a US citizen who genuinely lived outside the US — 330 full days and no US abode in at least one of the last three years. The domestic track caps the penalty at 5% of the highest aggregate balance. Compare that with 50% for a willful violation.
  6. Do not hold Chinese funds, money-market funds or wealth products if you are a US citizen. A Chinese mutual fund is very likely a PFIC, and the default §1291 regime spreads the gain across the whole holding period, taxes it at the top rate for each year, and adds an interest charge. QDII does not solve this — it wraps the same problem in a domestic product and does not move the money out.

6. One caution on the premise itself

The framing "money stuck in China with no way to convert it without paying considerable taxes" does not match the law. What does match the law is: money that is difficult to move in one step, because China has not opened the capital account, and that becomes very expensive to move unlawfully or to receive without filing.

The distinction is worth about 25–50% of the balance. It is the difference between a USD 1,897 spread and a USD 112,718 penalty on the same USD 450,870.
