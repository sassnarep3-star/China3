US inbound rules

The US does not tax a genuine gift or an inheritance received from China. What the US does impose is (a) information reporting with fixed and percentage penalties, and (b) income tax on income earned inside the Chinese account. The large penalties people actually experience are the information-return penalties in rows US-03 to US-05, not tax on the money.

The three numbers that matter most

USD 100,000 — the Form 3520 Part IV threshold for gifts from a foreign individual (not indexed). Failure to file is 5% of the gift per month, capped at 25%.

USD 10,000 — the FBAR threshold, measured as the aggregate of all foreign accounts at any point in the year. This applies to the Chinese account you already have, whether or not you move anything.

50% — the FBAR willful penalty, measured against the account balance rather than against any tax owed. Against 5% for the streamlined domestic procedure and 0% for the streamlined foreign one.
