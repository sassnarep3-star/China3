Category 02 · taxes are not the exchange rate

Break down the “penalty” before paying it

The crucial first question is whether a quoted deduction is tax, a bank’s FX spread/fee, a tax on the underlying income or asset sale, or a compliance hold. These have different legal bases and different ways to verify them.

What official sources support: the USD 50,000 individual FX convenience amount is not itself a tax. Official SAFE guidance describes it as a simplified processing threshold and says authentic eligible current-account transactions above it can be supported with transaction documents. The reviewed official sources do not establish a single flat tax percentage on moving an individual’s already-existing personal bank balance from CNY to USD.

The conversion deduction: calculate it without guessing

Market / bank FX cost

Gross USD = CNY amount ÷ quoted CNY per USD

Get the customer quote, not a reference/mid-market rate treated as guaranteed. Confirm whether the quote is CNY per USD or USD per CNY, and capture its exact timestamp.

FX cost vs chosen benchmark = gross USD at benchmark − gross USD at bank customer rate

A benchmark is only a comparison convention, not necessarily an available execution rate. No numeric result is possible without a quote and chosen benchmark.

All-in transfer cost

Net USD received = gross USD − sending fee − intermediary deductions − receiving fee − separately identified tax/withholding (if legally due)

Ask that each item be named and sourced. If a deduction is labeled “tax,” request the tax type, taxable base, legal provision, tax authority/withholding agent and official receipt or assessment. Do not treat an exchange spread or bank fee as income tax.

All fee, quote and personal tax values intentionally blank until independently documented.

Tax-credit caution

Foreign tax credit rules concern qualifying foreign income taxes on foreign-source income and are limited. Do not assume every Chinese charge, FX fee, wealth/property tax or remittance deduction can be credited against U.S. tax. IRS Publication 514 explains the qualification and limitation framework; individual application requires facts.
